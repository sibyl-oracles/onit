feat(learn): the ruler, the recall path, and the outcome signals

The three cheap deliverables of the build order, in one commit
because none of them is a loop on its own: the ruler that measures
them, the retrieval that feeds the model, and the labels that tell
the retrieval which episodes were worth retrieving.

**The ruler** (`src/learn/eval.py`).  A holdout drawn from the
recorded corpus, a dev split for iteration, and a pinned baseline --
all of them written *outside* the agent's writable surface, enforced by
`assert_outside_agent_surface()` rather than by convention.  I1 says
no loop reads the holdout; I7 says no loop can write it.  Together
they are the difference between a self-improving system and a
self-certifying one.

**The recall** (`src/learn/recall.py`).  BM25 over the trajectories,
reusing the retrieval kit from `toolkit.py`, ranked by the outcome
signal so the agent's own failures do not come back as lessons.  The
recalled episodes ride in the *volatile* half of the prompt, because
episodes are per-task and would thrash the prefix cache otherwise.

**The signals** (`src/learn/signals.py`, `src/ui/rating.py`).  The
corpus had 1,010 records and zero ratings; what it did have was 820
verifier labels and a body of runs whose outcome could be read off the
conversation -- a rephrase is a failure, an acceptance is a success.
The derived labels land in a file *beside* the trajectories, never
over them, so the audit trail survives.  `onit learn --signals`
reports how many records carry a usable label; `--write-signals`
materialises them.

`\rate` in the text UI is the other half: a rating path for the
interface that has one, so the corpus is not waiting on a human to
remember to click a button.
